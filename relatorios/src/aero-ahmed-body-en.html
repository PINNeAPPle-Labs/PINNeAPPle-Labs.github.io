AER-01 · Ahmed body1 · the case

The Ahmed body is the most used benchmark in automotive aerodynamics: a simple geometry with a slanted rear whose angle decides the drag. It was adopted because it is published, measured and hard — the flow separates, and that is where models fail.

ItemValue
Geometry1.044 × 0.389 × 0.288 m · 0.1 m front radii · 0.222 m rear slant, stilts left out
Modelhalf model, moving road, steady RANS k-ω SST with wall functions, 5 prism layers
Variablesslant 0 to 40° · ground clearance 30 to 80 mm · speed 20 to 60 m/s
CADOpenCASCADE B-rep through gmsh, re-imported from its STL: closed, symmetric to 1.0 mm, volume 0.10993 m³ against 0.10959 parametric

CFD on the CAD body at the reference condition (25°, 50 mm, 60 m/s) gave CD 0.3004 against 0.2966 for the parametric body — 1.3% of difference from going through CAD and back. That is the study's first uncertainty, and it appears before the first simulation.

The question of the study. It was not “what is the CD”: it was how much of the error is mesh and how much is model — because the two ask for opposite decisions. A mesh is refined; a model is replaced.

L0–L1–L2 converges oscillatorily; L1–L2–L3 converges monotonically, with observed order 4.26 and Richardson value 0.2617. The GCI on L3 is 1.1%.

Read the order with suspicion. 4.26 sits above the scheme's formal order (2), which makes the GCI a lower bound on numerical uncertainty — not a sharp estimate. Saying so is part of the result.

The campaign mesh (L1) sits 13.3% above the Richardson value. It was kept for cost: 7.5 min against 19 min on L2 and 107 min on L3, on 4 cores. That 13% is the numerical uncertainty of every surrogate trained on the campaign.

Below the critical angle, refining the mesh brings the CFD towards the measurement — the behaviour expected from a numerical error. That band is what holds up the rest of the study.

Where validation stops holding. From 30° up, refining makes it worse: the error grows from 10.6% to 15.8% and from 47.5% to 56.9%. That is not mesh noise — it is the model.

AER-01 · Ahmed body4 · the error that belongs to the model

Steady RANS with k-ω SST keeps the slant flow attached beyond 30°, where the experiment separates. The result is the drag peak moved from 30° to 35–40°.

It is an error of model physics, and the practical consequence is direct: no amount of mesh fixes it, and no surrogate trained on those cases learns the real behaviour — it learns the solver's bias, faithfully.

What that costs a project. Rear-geometry choices live exactly in the 25°–35° band. A study that only reports “CD = 0.32” there is handing over a number with up to 57% model error — without saying so.

AER-01 · Ahmed body5 · surrogate and sensitivity

Forty-eight geometries by maximin Latin hypercube (centred L2 discrepancy 0.0039 against 0.0141 for random designs of the same size); 48 of 48 ran. The final set has 54 cases: 28 train, 6 validation, 10 test and 10 extrapolation (every case with slant above 34°).

Relative error for CD and CL; mean relative L2 for the Cp field. The test and extrapolation splits are measured once, after model selection.

Sobol indices over the training region: on CD, slant explains 0.96 of the variance, clearance 0.05 and speed 0.01. For friction drag the order flips (0.09 / 0.04 / 0.93): two phenomena, two dependencies.

Extrapolation warns. Above 34° of slant, interval coverage drops to 0.6–0.8: the model is not to be used there, and the twin says so on screen instead of answering.

What this study does not solve

  • Steady RANS does not capture the unstable separation of the slant above 30°; that needs URANS or LES.
  • The campaign-mesh uncertainty (13.3%) is embedded in every surrogate trained on it.
  • The friction model fails the physical check “falls with Re” and was dropped — not for error, for physics.
  • y⁺ inside the wall-function range was required in every run; outside it, the result is not comparable.
