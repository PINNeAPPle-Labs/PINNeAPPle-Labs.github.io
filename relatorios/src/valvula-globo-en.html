VR-08 · globe valve, 80 → 60 bar1 · what arrived

A document arrived with a globe valve simulated in Ansys Fluent whose results, in the sender's words, “do not present a coherent physical result”, together with the CAD of the part.

Before running anything, the original case was diagnosed. Four of its numbers contradict each other:

#FindingThe numberWhy it does not hold
1physical time300 × 1e-7 s = 30 µsthe body's acoustic crossing is ≈ 0.83 ms: the run stopped at 3.6% of one crossing
2regimeMach 0.904with 80 → 60 bar the isentropic ceiling is 0.674; Mach 0.904 would need 48.3 bar at the outlet
3temperature209 to 367 Kisenthalpic throttling gives −8.12 K; 209 K would need Mach 1.4
4geometryplug 4 mm from the seatthe CAD shows the gap, and the field is not that of a 4 mm opening
Verdict in one line. The diagnosis of the original case is conclusive and needed no CFD: it stopped before the field existed. What came next is the field solved in physical time, with numbers in the right physical range.

Why the pressure ramps up

A single 80 bar step over a still domain does not close: the first pressure correction over-accelerates, the gas expands locally below the outlet pressure and the Peng-Robinson T(h) inversion blows up. The case raises stagnation in stages — 62 → 66 → 70 → 74 → 77 → 80 bar —, each warmed by the previous field, with a progressively less diffusive scheme.

What can already be decided

Flow, pressure drop and discharge coefficient at the evaluated opening are in the physical range: Mach 0.525, density within 0.10% of real gas and ΔT 0.3 K from the isenthalpic reference. For operation and for comparing alternatives, that number works — with the caveats beside it.

What cannot, yet

The characteristic curve against opening, and any purchase decision that depends on Kv better than 10%. For that, the seat curtain needs at least 6 cells radially.
