ELE-01 · 4-pole SPM machine1 · what was computed

A surface-mounted permanent-magnet machine in 2-D: cogging, torque against current angle, back-EMF and losses. Torque is computed twice, by independent routes, and both are printed side by side.

QuantityValue
Poles / slots2 pole pairs · 24 slots
Axial length · airgap50 mm · 1 mm
MagnetBr = 1.2 T, surface mounted
Angular discretisation360 positions · 2-D A_z FEM
Costabout 2 min per run
Why two routes. Maxwell stress and co-energy use different arithmetic on the same field. If they agree, the field is resolved enough for torque; if they diverge, the cause is usually the mesh or the angular discretisation — and the number should not ship.

At the design point the two routes sit 0.087% apart — far below anything a design decision can resolve.

At low-torque points the relative disagreement grows: at cogging instants it reaches 2.6% (0.3391 against 0.3302 Nm). Not a contradiction: the same absolute difference weighs more on a small number, which is why the check must also be read in absolute terms.

House rule. Two independent routes do not prove the result is right — they prove it is consistent. The proof against reality is still a bench test, and this report has none.

On the same formulation, a topology optimization of the reluctance rotor: 1,410 design variables over 5,640 elements, gradient checked by finite differences with a maximum relative error of 4.6·10⁻⁸.

The difference that changes the decision. The same design delivers 38.1 Nm in the linear model and 13.4 Nm with saturation — a factor of 2.8. The linear number is useful for exploring the design space; it is not a machine number.

Why publish a case without a test. Because internal consistency is verifiable and useful: a design that fails the Maxwell × co-energy check has a problem before it reaches the bench. What this report does not do is call consistency validation.

What can be decided with this study

Comparing rotor topologies, choosing the current point and sizing the magnet, with the consistency check in place. To buy or certify a machine, a bench test and thermal are still missing.
