EST-01 · topology optimization1 · the problem

Topology optimization answers a design question: where to put material so the structure is stiffer at the same mass. The answer is a density field, and what decides whether it is usable is the quality of that field — not the pretty picture.

CaseWhat it is
MBB beamclassic benchmark load case, maximum volume 50%
Cantilevercantilever beam, maximum volume 40%
FormulationSIMP with density filter and, in one run, projection
OptimizersOC (optimality criteria) and MMA (method of moving asymptotes)
What is measured. Compliance (lower is better), final volume fraction, grey element fraction and the gradient check error. The number that matters is the combination — low compliance with little grey.

EST-01 · topology optimization2 · method and check

Every run ends with three checks: the volume is the one requested, the grey fraction is measured, and the analytical gradient is compared against finite differences.

The gradient check looks like paperwork until the day a result improves 20% with no explanation: with it, either the gradient is right and the gain is real, or the optimizer is walking down the wrong slope — and that shows up before it becomes a drawing.

OC and MMA land in practically the same place with the filter (233.71 against 233.49), with MMA in 58 iterations against 146. Projection changes the result for real: compliance drops to 190.05, over 207 iterations.

The correct reading. It is not “projection is better”: projection pushes density towards 0 or 1, which changes the physics of the problem being solved. Comparing the two numbers without the grey fraction would be comparing two different structures.

EST-01 · topology optimization4 · the grey that stays

Without projection, 44% of the elements sit at intermediate density — a material that does not exist. With projection that fraction falls to 2.25% on the MBB and 0.88% on the cantilever.

MetricMBB with filterMBB with projectionEffect
Compliance233.49190.05−18.6%
Grey fraction0.4530.0225−95%
Iterations58207×3.6 cost

The 18.6% compliance gain arrives with 3.6 times more iterations. The design decision is that trade — not an obvious choice.

What can be decided

Where to put material, at what computational cost, and whether the result is trustworthy (gradient checked, volume respected, grey measured). What does not come out of here is a manufacturable part.
