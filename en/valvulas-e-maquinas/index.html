Where it hurts

The problem as it arrives here

Three pains that bring a flow study here — and what the number decides.

The catalogue gives the range, not your point

The vendor curve holds for water or standard air. In your gas, at your pressure, the regime is another one.

The sonic regime nobody checks

With gas at a high pressure drop, half the cases choke. Without that check, the solver result comes out pretty and wrong.

Optimization that starts on the bench

Testing five geometries experimentally costs months. The right order is to filter on the computer and test only the winner.

In and out

What we need and what you get

Without these the study does not start; with them, the number comes with its validity range stated.

In

  • Valve, nozzle, orifice or passage geometry (CAD/STEP), with stroke and opening
  • Fluid and equation of state (real gas, where that is the case)
  • Inlet and outlet pressure and temperature; expected flow rate
  • Wall material and roughness
  • What the decision needs: flow rate, pressure drop, force, noise or shape

Out

  • Mass flow and discharge coefficient at the evaluated opening
  • Pressure drop and pressure, temperature and Mach fields
  • Acoustic crossing check: a warning when the case is not representable
  • Mesh quality measured and recorded (non-orthogonality and checkMesh)
  • Report with the verification and the limits section
