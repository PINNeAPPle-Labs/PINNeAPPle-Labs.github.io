Surrogate model

A heat sink that answers 200,000 times faster

Seven heat-sink geometries, a 3D solver and a model that returns junction temperature in milliseconds. 4.46% mean error; 7.35% worst case.

30 July 2026 6 min read

Anyone who designs heat sinks knows the routine: every change of fin is a new 3D simulation. Seven variants become a week; seventy become the whole project.

What was done

Seven heat-sink geometries solved in 3D FEniCS/dolfinx, with meshes built in gmsh. Then a surrogate trained on those simulations and compared against the solver at points that were not in the training set, the reference is always external to the model.

The numbers

Around 200,000 times faster (the measured mean across the seven geometries was 227,059×, with the worst case at 57,222×). Mean error of 4.46%, worst case 7.35%. And the prediction is deterministic: three runs, on different days, returned the same numbers.

What it changes

Exploring the design space now fits inside one meeting. The solver is still called, to validate the choice, not to discover the optimum.

~200,000×faster, at 4.46% mean error
What was not proven: 4.46% is the mean inside the trained geometry range; outside it, the error is unmeasured. Determinism is not accuracy: the same numbers on every run say nothing about regions where there is no data.
