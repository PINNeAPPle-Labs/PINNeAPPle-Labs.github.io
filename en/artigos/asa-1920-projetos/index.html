Optimization

1,920 wings, three objectives and 13% less CO₂

Multi-objective optimization in 3D CFD: every wing was flown in OpenFOAM, and the result is not “the best wing”, it is the map of trade-offs between three objectives that fight each other.

20 September 2026 7 min read

Making an aircraft more efficient is not about minimising one number. Less CO₂ per passenger usually comes with a slower cruise or a faster approach; slowing the approach usually costs drag. The design is a negotiation, and a negotiation needs a map, not a single answer.

What was done

1,920 wing geometries generated and evaluated against three objectives at once: CO₂ per passenger-kilometre, cruise speed and approach speed. The evolutionary algorithm (NSGA-II) picks the next batch; a surrogate trained on the previous rounds guides the search; the candidates that survive are re-checked in 3D OpenFOAM before they enter the result.

The number

−13% CO₂ per passenger-kilometre across the solution set, with −18.2% profile drag and target lift held to within 0.04%.

How to read the result

The output is a set of wings, not one. Whoever wants less CO₂ accepts a different cruise; whoever needs a slow approach picks another point on the front. Optimization shows the price of each choice; engineering makes it.

−13% CO₂/pax-kmwith target lift held to 0.04%
What was not proven: Optimization does not prove the wing is manufacturable, structurally sound, or good outside the simulated conditions. What is measured is drag, lift and CO₂ under the study's conditions, checked in the 3D solver.
