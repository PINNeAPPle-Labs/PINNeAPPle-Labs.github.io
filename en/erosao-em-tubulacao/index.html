Where it hurts

The problem as it arrives here

Three pains that bring an erosion study here — and that the number either settles or bounds.

The unplanned shutdown

Erosion does not show up in routine inspection until it breaks through. By then the line is out and the shutdown is no longer planned.

The material bought “to be safe”

A thicker wall or a different alloy works — and costs. Without the rate, sizing becomes a bet on the expensive side.

The rule of thumb that is off by 100×

The standard's closed-form formula, without CFD, came out 97 to 100 times below the experiment in two of the three cases we checked.

In and out

What we need and what you get

Without these the study does not start; with them, the number comes with its validity range stated.

In

  • Geometry: CAD, STEP or a drawing with diameter, bend radius and runs
  • Fluid: flow rate, pressure and operating temperature
  • Particles: size, density, shape and concentration (or measured sand rate)
  • Wall material and hardness; initial and minimum allowable thickness
  • History: operating hours per year and shutdowns

Out

  • Wall erosion map, in mm/year, on your geometry
  • Peak position and value — where to measure thickness first
  • Service life down to the minimum thickness, with its uncertainty band
  • Sensitivity to flow rate, bend radius and material
  • Report with the experiment check and the limits section
